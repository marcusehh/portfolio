**What is sovereign AI?**
Sovereign AI is a nation-state's or region's capability to develop, deploy and govern AI using localised infrastructure. In 2026, it is no longer just data sovereignty (data being kept in the country), it is full-stack control from the compute to the deployment of AI. (McKinsey, 2026)

**Why do countries want sovereignty?**
The power to dictate a country's use of AI is increasingly in the hand of a few American and Chinese mega corporations, namely; Open AI, Anthropic and Nvidia. This means local power is limited over AI models. With sovereign AI, the power could become more decentralised and lead to governments having more control rather than a few private organisations. More than that, the US as a global leader of AI has the ability to weaponize their leadership. In the dying days of Joe Biden's presidency in 2025, America proposed sweeping export controls that would have rationed access to advanced chips through a complex licensing regime. (Economist, 2025)

As well as that, individuals, companies and governments are concerned about where their data is actually going and who has access. With AI held only within a specific country, the proposal is that there is less risk of confidential data being obtained by groups who shouldn't have access. However, even this may not be enough. - On the 10th June 2025, the Director of Public and Legal Affairs at Microsoft France said under oath that the company cannot guarantee French public-sector data will stay beyond the reach of US authorities. (The Register, 2025) This is because the 2018 CLOUD Act (the act that means US companies can be compelled to hand over data) reaches data held by US companies regardless of where it physically sits.

**Who's actually doing it?**
Canada has launched its own plan to reduce reliance on American technology providers. India, Japan and Singapore are backing domestic AI infrastructure models. The Center for a New American Society (CNAS), a think-tank in Washington, estimates that the number of state-backed AI projects outside America and China grew fivefold over the course of 2024 and 2025. Governments have announced investments of $70bn-plus in such initiatives, according to CNAS's tally. (Economist, 2025) The majority of sovereign AI models rely on open source models such as models produced by z.ai, a Chinese AI company. The problem is, what if China puts the same export controls on their future models as America?

**Can sovereign AI truly happen?**
Lawmakers are often behind the curve. One example is in the congressional hearing of TikTok on the 23rd March, 2023. There is a viral clip of Richard Hudson, a Republican Representative from North Carolina, asking the TikTok CEO, Shou Zi Chew, "Does TikTok access the home WIFI network". (Futurism, 2023) If the lawmakers meant to partner with business leaders cannot even grasp the internet, there is no chance that sovereignty will be possible in AI. While this is just one example and does not speak to every lawmaker, it suggests that the problem may be systemic.

Sovereign AI is where a specific country manages the full stack of AI, holding the data within its national borders and owning their own infrastructure. However, with technology it is exceedingly difficult to enforce national borders. The main reason for this is due to the fact that much of the compute is concentrated in the hands of two key players, the US and China. The US and China control 90% of the compute needed to develop and deploy cutting-edge AI and own all fifty top-ranked foundation models. Roughly 70% of tracked sovereign projects involve at least one foreign partner, and four-fifths of those involve a US company. (CNAS, 2026) Nvidia alone supplies GPUs for 52% of tracked infrastructure projects. (CNAS, 2026) The question is not whether full sovereign AI is achievable in the near future, since it is not, but whether countries can secure meaningful agency over deployment within their borders.
